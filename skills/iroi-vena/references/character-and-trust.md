# Character Psychology, Boundaries & the Trust Matrix
# Karakterek, határok és a bizalom dimenziói

This reference provides a structural framework for deep character psychology, interpersonal boundaries, multidimensional trust, and the ethics of care in serial fiction.

---

## 1. The Four Layers of Trust (A bizalom négy rétege)

Trust is not a binary switch („they trust each other” / „they don't”). It develops and breaks across four independent dimensions:

1. **Trust in Competence (Szakértelem):**
   - Belief that the other can execute a difficult task or keep a physical boundary intact.
   - Example: Characters can trust each other in an emergency while keeping private life completely secret.
2. **Trust in Honesty (Őszinteség):**
   - Belief that the other will state reality rather than flattering or concealing harsh truths.
3. **Comfort with Vulnerability (Sebezhetőség felvállalása):**
   - Willingness to appear exhausted, uncertain, flawed, or afraid without fearing contempt or weaponized exposure.
4. **Willingness to Accept Help (Segítség elfogadása):**
   - The hardest dimension: allowing the other to assist without feeling diminished, indebted, or controlled.

## 2. Care as Labor vs. Care as Control
## Gondoskodás mint munka vs. Gondoskodás mint irányítás

A recurring interpersonal dynamic across any close relationship—whether between comrades, partners, family members, or mentors—is the line between genuine support and covert control:

- **Care as Actionable Labor:**
  - Who notices exhaustion before it is announced?
  - Who takes on the dirty, dangerous, or unglamorous tasks?
  - Genuine support carries a **real cost**: time, energy, sacrifice, or personal risk.
- **When Care Becomes Control:**
  - Deciding for another person without their consent under the guise of „protecting” them.
  - Converting assistance into an emotional debt (*„After all I've done for you...”*).
  - Authentic relationships (alliances, partnerships, or mentorships) honor the other person's right to take calculated risks, maintain privacy, and make independent choices.

## 3. Character Snapshot Architecture (Karakterpillanatkép szerkezete)

Before drafting an emotionally charged scene, define the focal character's state:

| Field | Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Immediate Want** | What tangible objective are they pursuing in this room right now? |
| **Active Risk** | What do they stand to lose if they fail or hesitate? |
| **Enduring Value** | What core conviction do they protect at all costs? |
| **Useful Competence** | What specific skill or insight can they bring to this obstacle? |
| **Blind Spot** | What habit, fear, or assumption distorts their view of the situation? |
| **Independent Boundary** | What line will they refuse to cross, even for a loved one or authority? |

---

## 4. Growth, Apology, and the Real Cost of Repair
## Hibák, bocsánatkérés és valódi jóvátétel

Character growth is proven through changed choices under comparable pressure, not through speeches:

- **An apology does not compel forgiveness:** The injured party is entitled to maintain distance, caution, or grief.
- **The Cost of Repair:** Genuine amends cost something: comfort, status, time, pride, or resources.
- A later revelation about a character's good intentions does not retroactively erase the pain they inflicted. Keep ethical consequences realistic.
