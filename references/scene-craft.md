# Scene Craft, Narrative Pacing & Pacing Architecture
# Jelenetépítés, dramaturgia és fejezettempó

This reference guides real-time scene construction, physical friction, the balance between scene and summary, and multi-character dramaturgical pacing.

---

## 1. Setting with a Job (A környezetnek munkát adni)

A scene should never consist of disembodied talking heads floating in an empty room. The physical setting must impose friction, tasks, and sensory obstacles on the characters.

- **Work-Bound Activity:** Characters must be occupied with physical manipulation: measuring steam pressure, scraping dough, cleaning spark plugs, folding damp laundry, wiping an ink pen, untangling a leather strap.
- **Friction Forces Decisions:** A jammed drawer, a cooling stove, a rusted valve, or a dying phone battery forces immediate physical choices while emotional stakes unfold.
- **No Room Catalogues:** Avoid listing static furniture unless a character interacts with, trips over, or hides something behind it.

---

## 2. Scene vs. Summary Dynamics (Jelenet és Összefoglalás egyensúlya)

A long-form narrative modulates between real-time dramatization and elegant narrative compression.

| Mode | Function | Time Ratio | Primary Language Tools | When to Use? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scene (Jelenet)** | Visceral dramatic immersion, micro-tension, subtext. | Real-time (1:1) or slowed down. | Dialogue dashes, physical friction, tactile/auditory detail, short sentences. | Turning points, confrontations, intimacy, catastrophe, revelations. |
| **Summary (Összefoglalás)** | Temporal bridge, context building, cumulative change. | Compressed (weeks, months in paragraphs). | Iterative verbs (`-gat/-get`, `-drog/-dreg`), temporal markers, metonymic wear. | Travel, convalescence, repetitive work, slow emotional erosion. |
| **The Pivot (Átmenet)** | Zoom-in / Zoom-out transition. | Smooth modulation. | Broad temporal pattern narrowing down to a single physical object or gesture. | Transitioning from summary into an active scene. |

### The Rules of Elegant Compression:
1. **Iterative Morphology:** Utilize Hungarian iterative suffixes: `-gat/-get` (*javítgatott, nézegetett*), `-kál/-kél` (*járkált*), `-kodik/-kedik` (*fagyoskodott*).
2. **Metonymic Detail Wear:** Show the passage of time through the erosion of physical matter (diminishing coal piles, ink levels dropping in the bottle, snow crust graying along fences), NEVER through abstract claims like *„a kapcsolatuk mélyült”*.

---

## 3. Eliminating the Choreographed Puppet Show (Gesztusinfláció felszámolása)

AI models reflexively insert mechanical micro-gestures after every dialogue line (*„bólintott”, „felsóhajtott”, „megvonta a vállát”, „összehúzta a szemöldökét”*).

### The Anti-Inflation Rules:
1. **The Pure Dash Rule:** If the speaker is obvious from context, omit both the speech tag and the gesture. Let dialogue carry its own rhythm.
2. **One Action per Block:** In an exchange, allow at most ONE concrete physical action, and it must belong to the task or environment (e.g., scraping mud from a boot, checking a dial), not gratuitous facial gymnastics.

---

## 4. Distributing Expertise (A szakértelem elosztása)

A cast becomes believable when problems cannot be solved by a single omniscient protagonist.

- Assign each participant an independent competence, a blind spot, and a distinct stake.
- The intellectual character should not spot every practical detail; the quiet or humorous character may recognize the decisive physical anomaly.
- **Test:** If the resolution would not change were any participant removed, redistribute the scene's critical choices.

---

## 5. Aftermath as Plot (Utóhatás mint cselekmény)

Narrative progression does not require inventing a new crisis in every scene. The aftermath of an ordeal carries immense dramatic momentum:

- Who doubts the account?
- Who is injured, who cleans up the debris?
- How has accountability shifted?
- What new promises were extracted, and what did they cost?
- Convalescence and restoration provide authentic, resonant story progression.

---

## 6. Social Scenes with Incompatible Agendas

A dinner, ball, council meeting, or family gathering must contain competing private objectives:

- Define what each character wants privately versus what public constraints prevent them from saying.
- Let characters speak past each other, misunderstand silences, and depart with different interpretations.
- Do not force artificial consensus at the end of an ensemble scene.

---

## 7. Romance & Character Agency

Authentic romantic tension relies on mutual autonomy:

- **The Right of Refusal:** Both partners must have independent wants and the demonstrated ability to say no, close a door, or challenge a decision.
- **Vulnerability is Not Helplessness:** Admitting pain, uncertainty, or exhaustion does not convert a character into a passive dependent.
- **Competence Outside the Relationship:** A character's self-worth and capabilities must be visible in actions independent of the partner.
- **Subtle Intimacy:** Closeness is established through shared work, quiet domestic observation, and restrained tactile specificity—not theatrical vows.
